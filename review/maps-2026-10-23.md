# Web map review, 23 October 2026

The ten lesson web maps were run through the map checker, then reviewed by the six personas in `review/personas.md`, each seeing only what a teacher or student would see. Every finding kept below was checked against the live web map, its layers or a published source. Findings that came from the way the maps were captured are listed under "Dismissed", so they can't be mistaken for problems with the maps.

Fix-list numbers for the sandbox maps were added later on 23 October, when the fixes went into `maps/fixes.md` (A9, A10, B5 to B14, C7 to C10).

Owner: **MapMaker** web maps are Revolutionary Battles, Roads, US Slavery 1850, Civil War and Vicksburg. **Sandbox** (GIS-4-Schools) web maps are We the People, Rights, Presidential Elections, Louisiana Purchase and War of 1812. **Lesson** means the fix is in the sheet, lesson page or answer notes. "Listed" gives the item in `maps/fixes.md` that already covers it.

Reviewers: T1 (Mark and Dana together), T3 Luis, T4 Ana, T5 Ruth, S1 Jordan, S2 Sofía. "6/6" means all six noticed it.

**What the checker missed**, found by drawing each map and clicking what the sheets name: colony pop-ups off (Revolutionary Battles); the Missouri Compromise Line's pop-up off; "39.099998474121094%" and "11/21/1789, 12:00 AM" (We the People); the "< 0" casualty class (Civil War, War of 1812); pop-ups that exist where the lessons say there are none (Key Port Cities, Indigenous Territories); and everything in the persona review below that depends on how the map looks at a bookmark.

**Suggested for the checker's next version**
- Read each layer item's pop-up, symbols, filter and visible range before judging, as the Maps SDK does: most MapMaker maps inherit them, so the web map alone gives both false alarms and misses (Civil War railroads, Missouri Compromise Line).
- Compare classes with their transparency and line style, not colour alone.
- Drop the "legend headed by a raw field" rule for single-field unique-value renderers: the Maps SDK legend doesn't show the field.
- Check number and date formats in pop-ups (decimal places, a time on a date).
- Flag size legends with a "< 0" or other impossible class.
- Count a field as shown only when the pop-up actually displays it.
- Flag point symbols drawn on top of another layer's points within a few pixels (Turning Points).

### Across the lessons
- **Selection highlight** fills a clicked feature with cyan, so its colour changes while the pop-up is open (Revolutionary Battles, US Slavery 1850, Louisiana Purchase). Check whether the app lets the highlight be an outline; the Result line (B1) covers the battle case.
- **Legend panel** covers part of several bookmarks at laptop size (Slavery, Louisiana, War of 1812). The pictures used the SDK legend in a panel on the right; check the same bookmarks in the app before moving them.
- **Spanish sheets**: give the English term the map uses the first time it appears: Bookmarks, casualties, seats, the legend group names (S2 and T4 on every map).

## Dismissed: capture artefacts

- **Blank battle pictures** (Revolutionary Battles, Civil War, Vicksburg): the pictures are on battlefields.org, which this workspace can't reach. Not tested on a school network.

## Method and limits
- Checker: MapMaker Web Map Auditor (`jsawle/mapchecker`, rubric v0.7.1), run headless on the ten web maps with `review/tools/run-mapchecker.mjs` (Checker runner 1.0). Export: `review/maps-2026-10-23/checker.json`.
- Capture: `review/tools/map-capture.mjs` (Map capture 1.0, ArcGIS Maps SDK for JavaScript 4.34, headless Chromium, 1200 × 800, legend in a panel on the right) followed each sheet's steps on the live maps: 61 views with legend and pop-up text, in `capture.json`.
- Personas: `review/build_map_packets.py` (Map review packets v1.0) gave each reviewer only the sheets, the lesson page for teachers, and the pictures. Twelve separate reviews (six personas, two groups of five maps), 381 findings (17 "stops the lesson", 205 "slows it down", 159 "minor"), in `T*.json` and `S*.json`.
- Limits: the pictures come from the Maps SDK, not the Atlas app, whose panels and highlight may differ. Nothing was tested on a Chromebook or a school network. The reviewers are personas, not teachers or students; a count says how many noticed a problem, not how much it matters in class. Findings marked "to check" or "to test" were not confirmed.
