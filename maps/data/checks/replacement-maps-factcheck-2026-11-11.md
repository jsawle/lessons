# Replacement maps fact-check: Civil War 1863 and Slavery 1850 tables (11 Nov 2026)

Files checked (not edited): `maps/data/civil-war-1863/states-1863.json` and `data.json`; `maps/data/slavery-1850/units-1850.json`, `routes-1850.json`, `data.json` and `routes.data.json`. The built GeoJSON properties were compared with the tables and carry the same text.

Method: every claim was checked against a source fetched on 11 Nov 2026 (listed per row). Britannica was not used. Wikipedia was used only to find leads. The Harper's plate itself was read from Esri's public scan tiles (`US_1850_Original_Map_Albers` tile service, the "US 1850 Original Map" layer of the "US Slavery and Slave Trade, 1850" web map, item 819f60a5a3344d958d24f505849abaaa), including its legend. loc.gov could not be reached (not on the session allowlist), so the Library of Congress item page was not seen.

No date, admission year, status, group or count was found wrong. All eleven secession dates, the 35-state set (19 + 5 + 7 + 4), the 16 free / 15 slave count for late 1850, all 18 admission years, and the Amistad and Creole accounts are confirmed. The one "wrong" item is the name given to the small "Unidentified" area marked open to slavery; the rest are wording.

**The three "Unidentified" areas, worked out from the plate legend:**

- Source areas 55 and 57 (table key `X-FREE`) carry the plate's class "Free by the act of Congress admitting Texas" and its "1845" label. They are the land Texas claimed north of 36°30' and gave up in 1850 that was not put into New Mexico or Utah Territory: the strip that later became the Oklahoma Panhandle, land south of the Arkansas River in today's Kansas and Colorado, and the northern end of the Texas claim in today's Colorado and Wyoming. In late 1850 it was unorganized. Treating it as free is defensible and follows the source. The legal basis is thinner than the label suggests: the 1845 resolution barred slavery in any state later formed from Texas land north of the line; no act said anything about the land once Texas ceded it.
- Source area 61 (table key `X-OPEN`) carries the plate's class "Free by Missouri Compromise of 1820, opened to slavery by Compromise of 1850" (solid dark green in the legend). The plate does not name it. My reading, an inference from the Utah act's boundary ("on the east by the summit of the Rocky Mountains"), is that it is Louisiana Purchase land west of the mountain crest that the 1850 act placed inside Utah Territory. It is north of 38°N so it cannot be New Mexico Territory, and only the Utah and New Mexico acts "opened" anything in 1850. So it was organized (Utah Territory), not unorganized. Treating it as open to slavery is defensible; calling it "Unorganized land" is not. Note the digitized patch sits at about 105.2-106.0°W, which on a modern basemap is the east side of the Front Range (Boulder, Estes Park); the real area west of the crest is further west. That is a position error in the 1920 map or its digitization.

Slavery 1850 (`units-1850.json`, `data.json`)

15. Counts: 31 states, 16 free and 15 slave, after California; 8 territory areas in the table. CRS table.
16. The source has 16 legend classes. Plate legend (9 free, 7 slave).
17. Admission years ME 1820, VT 1791, OH 1803, IN 1816, IL 1818, MI 1837, WI 1848, IA 1846, CA 1850, FL 1845, AL 1819, MS 1817, LA 1812, TX 1845, AR 1836, TN 1796, KY 1792, MO 1821 (18 items); the 13 with no year are the original states. CRS table.
18. Free states other than NJ, "Slavery was banned here." Plate legend classes (abolition by state action, Ordinance of 1787, state constitutions). I did not check each state's abolition law separately.
19. ME: free state under the Missouri Compromise, 15 March 1820. CRS table.
20. CA: free state, 9 Sept 1850, part of the Compromise of 1850. https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/compromise-of-1850
21. The 15 slave states and DC, "Slavery was legal here." Plate legend; 1850 status as in the CRS and NARA pages.
22. VA includes today's West Virginia until 1863. https://www.archives.gov/legislative/features/west-virginia
23. TX annexed as a slave state in 1845 (resolution 1 March, admitted 29 Dec 1845). https://avalon.law.yale.edu/19th_century/texan01.asp
24. MO admitted 1821 (10 Aug) as a slave state under the Missouri Compromise; north-west corner added in 1837. https://sos.mo.gov/archives/history/timeline/2
25. OR-T: organized 14 Aug 1848 with slavery banned (section 14 applies the Ordinance of 1787's prohibitions). https://www.govinfo.gov/link/statute/9/323
26. MN-T: organized 3 March 1849; free. The act does not mention slavery; it continues Wisconsin Territory's laws, and the plate classes the area free by the Ordinance of 1787 and the Missouri Compromise. https://sos.mn.gov/media/2297/organicact.pdf
27. UNORG: closed to slavery by the Missouri Compromise. Plate legend ("Free by Missouri Compromise of 1820 only").
28. UT-T and NM-T: organized by the Compromise of 1850 with slavery left to the settlers (2 items). The acts say each may be admitted as a state "with or without slavery, as their constitution may prescribe"; "left for the settlers to decide" is the usual shorthand and is acceptable. New Mexico's act came into force only after Texas accepted its boundary (25 Nov 1850; President's message 13 Dec 1850), which is still "late 1850". https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/compromise-of-1850
29. IT: not an organized territory; slavery legal among several nations moved there in the 1830s (the Cherokee, Chickasaw, Choctaw, Creek and Seminole brought enslaved people with them; about 10,000 by 1861). https://www.okhistory.org/publications/enc/entry?entry=FR016
30. Status labels 1-4. Plate legend and the sources above.
