# Independent fact-check: two grade 8 US History lesson drafts

Date: 11 November 2026 (UTC). Lessons checked, not edited:

- CW = `civil-war-battlefields.json` (0.8.0), map "The Civil War, 1863"
- SL = `us-slavery-and-slave-trade-1850.json` (0.9.0), map "Slavery and the slave trade, 1850"

## How it was checked

- Every layer was downloaded from the live services with `/query?where=1=1&outFields=*&outSR=4326&f=geojson` (railroads with `where=InOpBy<=1863`, paged, 16,137 segments) and recounted in Python (shapely, pyproj WGS84 geodesics). Scripts and data are in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-claude/d904fd02-344e-50bf-8aad-bd30cd0dbfa1/scratchpad/fc/`.
- Map wording was compared with `/home/claude/lessons/maps/specs/civil-war-1863.json` and `slavery-1850.json`, the built `webmap.json` files and the build test screenshots in `/home/claude/lessons/maps/build/*/test/`.
- Historical claims were checked against pages fetched in this session. No Britannica; Wikipedia used for one lead only (Delaware) and not relied on.
- Limits: the two web map items could not be read (HTTP 403, private), so pop-ups, legend and bookmarks were checked from the spec, the build output and the screenshots, not from the live web map. Some pages could not be fetched from this environment; they are listed as "could not verify", not guessed.

Slavery lesson, history:

62. Missouri Compromise, 1820: slavery prohibited north of 36°30′ in the land ceded by France, Missouri excepted, Maine admitted as a free state to keep the balance (https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/missouri-compromise).
63. California admitted 9 September 1850; Utah and New Mexico organized the same day "with or without slavery, as their constitution may prescribe" (https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/compromise-of-1850). "Left for settlers to decide" is the usual shorthand.
64. New Jersey: the 1846 act abolished slavery and made each enslaved person "an apprentice, bound to service to his or her present owner" (https://www.njstatelib.org/research_library/new_jersey_resources/highlights/african_american_history_curriculum/unit_5_antebellum_america); 236 counted as slaves in 1850 (Census Bureau table at https://teachingamericanhistory.org/document/number-of-slaves-in-the-territory-enumerated-1790-to-1850/).
65. La Amistad: Africans "kidnapped and transported illegally" seized the ship in July 1839; it was seized off Long Island and taken to New London; the Supreme Court freed them on 9 March 1841 (https://www.archives.gov/education/lessons/amistad; https://www.loc.gov/item/today-in-history/march-09/).
66. Creole: left Richmond in October 1841 with 135 people to be sold in New Orleans; revolt 7 November 1841; sailed to the Bahamas; most were treated as free, five went on to New Orleans (https://docsteach.org/document/manifest-brig-creole/).
